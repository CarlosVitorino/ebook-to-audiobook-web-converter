# narrator.guru — Ebook-to-Audiobook Web Converter
## What this is

Upload an EPUB or PDF, get back a narrated audiobook with captions The result makes this newly possible: Readers with visual impairments, commuters, and people with dyslexia own ebooks they bought legally but cannot convert to audio without a terminal and Python. Self-published authors want audio versions of their own books without paying a narrator or an Audible distribution fee. Today they either give up, pay ACX narrators hundreds of dollars, or ask a technical friend. The engine already does this; the gap is a web form.

## What would make you stop

copywrite issues. We need a monetizing strategy. Like 1 free, next you need to buy like a pack, something like that.

## Who it is for

Readers with visual impairments, commuters, and people with dyslexia who own legally purchased ebooks they cannot convert to audio without technical tools, plus self-published authors who want audio versions of their own books without paying a professional narrator hundreds or thousands of dollars.

## The smallest thing that proves demand

A simple web form where a user uploads an EPUB or PDF and receives back a finished whole-book M4B file with synced captions, built on the existing open-source engine, with a first-conversion-free pricing message. If people will pay for a second book after their first free conversion, the thesis holds.

## What this is not building

- Dashboards or libraries beyond the single conversion flow (accounts exist only to sign in and hold credits; decided 2026-10-02)
- Voice cloning or custom voices (picking one of a few built-in Kokoro voices is allowed; decided 2026-10-02)
- Mobile apps
- DRM-protected ebook handling
- Distributed audiobook publishing or store integrations
- Subscription billing plans (start with free-first-conversion + paid packs)

## Who already serves this

Three free browser tools serve the need but stop short of a finished product: koby.luarai.com is fully client-side with no upload or signup but does not produce a single-file M4B of a whole book; Yaps and OfflineTTS likewise deliver in-browser audio without a completed whole-book file with captions. Above them sit professional narration at $1,200–$7,000 per standard title and a $5,000 quote for a 90,000-word novel, plus subscription converters charging monthly for what is really a one-shot job. No one currently offers a finished whole-book M4B with captions at a fair per-book price.

## What this tracks

The product keeps track of uploads, each holding the source file (EPUB or PDF), its format, word or character count, and an ownership/copyright acknowledgment from the user. Each upload maps to a conversion job with a status, the voice used, and on completion an output M4B file with its synced caption track. Users are tracked minimally, tied to their free-first-conversion status and to conversion packs purchased, with a count of remaining paid conversions on each pack. Revenue records link packs to payments.

## How the first users arrive

The notes on file show where the target users already gather — the free competition was itself discovered via a Reddit r/SideProject post, and the space has an active content ecosystem of 'best converter' guide articles ranking tools. First users are expected to arrive by posting where those readers and self-published authors already look for converters and by being listed or compared in the same guides, with the finished-M4B-with-captions-plus-fair-price wedge as the hook.
